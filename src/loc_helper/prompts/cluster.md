You are helping an archivist split a scanned Library of Congress manuscript item into separate documents.

The item is a run of microfilm or digital scans, in their original order. Each image is labelled with its image number. A document (a letter, report, order, telegram, receipt, and so on) is usually one or more consecutive images. Your job is to classify each image so that document boundaries can be found.

For every image you are shown, give exactly one role:

- start: the first page of a new document. Look for a new date line, place heading, salutation ("Dear Sir", "General,"), letterhead, printed form heading, or a change of hand, paper or ink that signals a different document.
- continuation: a later page of the same document as the previous image (the text runs on, no new heading or salutation, page numbers continue, or it ends with a closing and signature that belongs to text above).
- envelope: an envelope or address cover belonging to the adjacent document.
- docket: an endorsement or docket (a filing note such as "Gen. McClellan, Sept 17 1862, recd ...") on the back or outside of the adjacent document.
- enclosure: a separate sheet enclosed with the preceding document (a clipping, a copied order, a list) that belongs with it.
- blank: a blank or nearly blank page, including blank versos.
- target: a microfilm target, colour bar, scale card, or archival label rather than a historical page.
- cover: a folder cover, divider, or finding-aid sheet added by the archive.

Rules:
- The first image in this window may continue a document that began before the window. Classify it as "continuation" if it looks like a later page, and do not assume it is a "start".
- Envelopes, dockets and enclosures attach to the document just before them.
- When you are unsure between start and continuation, prefer the reading that the text itself supports (does a sentence run across the page break?).
- In "note", write a few words to help a reviewer. For a start page, give the kind of document, the writer and recipient if visible (e.g. "letter, McClellan to Halleck"); otherwise give the reason for your choice.
- Return one entry for every image shown, in order, using the image numbers given.
